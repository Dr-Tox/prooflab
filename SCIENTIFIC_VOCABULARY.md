# Scientific vocabulary and notation provenance

The explicit term list in `src/vocabulary.ts` is original ProofLab curation of common scientific terminology, laboratory methods, molecular formulas, acronyms, and selected identifier examples. It is not copied from a commercial dictionary, biomedical database, Microsoft Word, or another project's vocabulary. No third-party lexicon is imported or downloaded at runtime. No external vocabulary license applies to this original list; it does not confer rights to any future imported resource. Harper's bundled dictionary and WASM retain their existing Apache-2.0 license and notices. User dictionary entries remain separate user-approved data.

The list is intentionally small and auditable. Each entry has a term, case policy, and terminology/identifier type. Common lexical terms accept capitalization at the beginning of a sentence; identifiers preserve exact case and Greek characters. Case variants get manual scientific review rather than unrelated everyday substitutions. Prefix matches are insufficient: `supernatant` does not accept arbitrary extensions. Curated entries include explicit inflections, not a rule accepting every unfamiliar scientific-looking word.

A possible technical misspelling is proposed only when Harper already reports a spelling problem, the token is at least seven letters, the first three letters match, edit distance is at most two, and the nearest curated term is unique. This is a suggestion requiring author acceptance, not scientific identity validation. Unknown terms remain eligible for spelling diagnostics; unknown identifier-like tokens get manual review instead of potentially destructive replacements.

Contributors should include the positive term, common misspelling, case variants, and an unrelated negative example when adding entries. Review term type/case and actual raw Harper output. Do not import large vocabularies without recording their source version, redistribution license, notices, and scientific scope. Do not use the held-out benchmark to choose terms or tune distance thresholds.

SI notation rules refer to the [BIPM SI Brochure](https://www.bipm.org/en/publications/si-brochure): a space separates the value and unit symbol (including °C); kelvin uses K without °. Ordinary and nonbreaking spaces are accepted. Unicode µ/μ, minus signs, and exponent symbols are preserved. Journal-specific `ml`/`mL` preferences are optional; no universal grammar error is asserted. Percent/statistical notation and numerical values are preserved according to the application's stated domain conventions. The implementation is a selected notation checker, not a dimensional-analysis or chemical/gene parser.

V2 adds explicitly curated `RNA-seq` and `CYP3A4` identifiers from the supplied scientific safety requirements. They follow the existing case-sensitive policy and original-curation provenance; unknown lookalike identifiers are still reviewed. SI-prefix recognition and relative centrifugal force belong to measurement handling rather than the personal dictionary.

V3 adds explicitly curated material/reagent nouns (microbead, microsphere, microcapsule, nanoparticle, nanosphere, microdroplet, hydrogel, liposome, cryovial and immunobead) with their regular plurals. These are original factual term entries, not an imported lexicon or a scientific-looking-word exemption. Misspelled technical forms remain detectable.
